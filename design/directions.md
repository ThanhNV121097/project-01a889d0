# Bốn Chân — three directions

Three ways to design the same clinic, shown to the owner as cards. The owner
chose **3, "The day on one screen"**; it is what the site is built in.

---

## Direction 1 — The price is on the door

**Colours** `#F7F3EC` ground · `#FFFFFF` surface · `#171310` ink · `#5B544C`
ink-soft · `#0B5D3B` accent · `#FFFFFF` accent-ink

**Faces** display Fraunces (variable, OFL) · body Inter. A display face that is
not a sans — because this is a two-person neighbourhood practice and the price
list is the headline; a serif reads as a notice in the window rather than as a
product launch.

**Scale** h1 54px / 1.02, tracking −0.025em · h2 34px · prices 21px tabular ·
labels 11px, 0.14em letterspacing. Low, because the first screen has to carry
all seven services with their durations and prices.

**First screen** Left two thirds: the label, a seven-word headline and the
whole price ledger — service, duration, price, a hairline between each. Right
third: one documentary photograph bleeding top to bottom off the right edge.

**Why it suits you** Your owners want to know what a visit costs and how long
it takes before they pick a day, and you would rather win them with a plain
price list than with a mood.

## Direction 2 — A quiet room for a frightened cat

**Colours** `#14110F` ground · `#1D1916` surface · `#F2EBE3` ink · `#A3988C`
ink-soft · `#E8A33D` accent · `#14110F` accent-ink

**Faces** display Bricolage Grotesque (variable, OFL) · body Inter. A grotesk
with real character rather than a neutral sans, so the headline carries a
voice at 68px on a dark ground.

**Scale** h1 68px / 1.02, tracking −0.03em · h2 40px · service lines 17px with
21px tabular prices · labels 11px, 0.16em.

**First screen** Two dark studio animal portraits bleeding off the left and
right edges — a wide one and a narrow strip — with the headline, the two vets'
names and three services with their times and prices in the lit column
between them.

**Why it suits you** Most of your owners come in worried — the cat that hates
the carrier, the dog going under for a dental — and what you really sell is
Dr Linh's hands and a calm room.

## 3. The day on one screen — CHOSEN

**Colours** `#EDF0F2` ground · `#FFFFFF` surface · `#0B1016` ink · `#54616E`
ink-soft · `#1F49E0` accent · `#FFFFFF` accent-ink

**Faces** display Space Grotesk (OFL) · body Inter with tabular figures.

**Scale** h1 40px / 1.05, tracking −0.02em · h2 24px · table rows 16px with
18px tabular prices · labels 11px, 0.12em. Deliberately the smallest of the
three — the screen's job is to hold seven services, two vets and their hours
above the fold.

**First screen** A full-bleed band of two clinic photographs across the top,
with the booking panel — step one, the services with duration and price; step
two, the two vets and the days they work — overlapping up into it.

**Why it suits you** What you actually want from a website is fewer telephone
calls: owners who book the right twenty-minute slot themselves, and a desk
that can see the day without paper.

---

## Notes that hold for all three

- The first screen carries the seven services with their stated durations and
  prices, the two vets and what each is for, the opening hours and the street.
- No telephone number, no address number, no reviews, no claims — none were
  given, and none was invented.
- The photographs are stand-ins. The clinic has none of its own yet; whichever
  direction wins, these are the shots to replace.
